🎨 Best surface: paint the wall (10×10)

👍 A smooth, flat-painted wall beats a cheap foldable screen — no seams, no waves, no wrinkles. In your blackout room a good flat white wall looks excellent for a fraction of a real screen's cost.
🕳️ A · Patch the TV-mount holes (no mesh needed)
🔩 TV-mount anchor holes are the easy kind — small, no real damage. Skip the mesh patches; a good spackle fills these fine.

3M Patch Plus Primer (spackle + primer in one)Best pick — fills the holes and primes, so patches won't flash. No separate primer needed.
…or DAP DryDex spackle (pink→white)Fine alternative — just add a dab of spot primer after

  1. Press spackle into each hole, overfilling slightly. For an anchor hole with a small void, do two passes — thin fill, let dry, then top it off so it doesn't sink.
  2. Let it dry (DryDex goes from pink to white when it's ready).
  3. Sand flush & smooth with 220; wipe the dust.
  4. If you used plain spackle (not the primer-in-one), dab spot primer on each patch.

⚠️ Two rules that make or break it:
1. FLAT finish only — never satin/eggshell/semi-gloss. Any sheen makes a bright hotspot glare that ruins projection.
2. Bright flat WHITE — gray "screen paint" needs a very bright projector; yours looks best on white in a blackout room.
  1. Tape the edges, lay a drop cloth.
  2. Prime if you're covering a dark color; let dry.
  3. Roll 2 thin coats of flat white in smooth vertical passes — keep texture minimal.
  4. Light 220 sand between coats for a glass-smooth surface, then wipe dust.
  5. Project onto it — a taut, smooth, flat-white wall in the dark looks great.
💎 Premium option: a dedicated projector-screen paint kit gives slightly better contrast & a tuned neutral tone — worth it if you want the absolute best, but a quality flat white is 90% of the result for a fraction of the price.

🖼️ 1 · (Alternative) Hang the screen

  1. Wall: the 8-ft (short) front wall. Your 64"-wide screen leaves ~16" clearance each side — centered, it looks intentional.
  2. Height: bottom edge ~24–30" off the floor so seated eye-level lands in the lower third of the picture.
  3. Mount: use the included adhesive hooks / grommet ties; pull it taut top and bottom to kill wrinkles (it's wrinkle-free but tension = flat = sharp).
  4. Black backing faces the wall — that's what stops light bleeding through and washing out the image.

📽️ 2 · Place the projector

Because your projector has auto-focus & auto-keystone, placement is forgiving — but distance sets the image size. Use the calculator to get in the ballpark, then nudge until the picture exactly fills the 62.8"-wide screen.
🔺 Your plan: tripod mount (you already have one) — perfect for this room. It's the most flexible option and needs zero drilling; you just slide it to the right distance and raise it above head height.
  1. Position: set the tripod just behind (or beside) the recliner so no one's head blocks the beam. With ~11 ft of depth you have room to get the throw distance right.
  2. Height: raise the projector above seated head height and aim it at the middle of the screen. Shooting slightly downward from above is exactly what the auto-keystone is for.
  3. Level it: use the tripod head to get it square & level to the screen — small tilt is fine, auto-keystone squares the image.
  4. Fill the screen: slide the tripod forward/back until the bright image exactly covers the 62.8"-wide white area, then let auto-focus sharpen.
  5. Stabilize: spread the legs wide, and if the floor's uneven set it on a firm surface — a mini projector is light and a bumped tripod = a shaking picture.
🔌 Cable safety: your two doors are walkways, so run the power (and AUX) cable along a wall and tape it down — don't stretch it across the aisle where you'd trip in the dark. 🔎 Don't know the exact throw ratio? Start ~6.5–8 ft back, project, and slide the tripod until it fits (mini projectors are usually ~1.2–1.5:1) — the calculator above gets you close.

  1. Soundbar under the screen, centered; subwoofer on the floor front-left or -right (bass is non-directional, so exact spot is flexible).
  2. Set the projector's audio output to your chosen method (AUX/BT) and turn its internal speaker down so you only hear the soundbar.
  3. If dialogue lags the picture, use the projector's audio-delay / lip-sync setting — AUX cable avoids this entirely.

✨ 4 · Make it look/feel great

  1. Go dark, not just window-less. Paint the front wall & nearby ceiling matte dark grey/black (or hang dark fabric). Light bouncing off a white wall next to the screen is what kills contrast — you've removed windows, so kill the reflections too.
  2. Ventilation (important, no windows): an 88 sq ft sealed room + projector heat + bodies = stuffy fast. Run a quiet fan, crack a door, or add a small vent. Keep the projector's intake/exhaust clear.
  3. Acoustics: small hard rooms echo. A rug, the couch, and a couple of soft panels/blankets on the side walls tighten the sound a lot for cheap.
  4. Seating: a single row ~7 ft from a 72" screen fills a comfortable field of view for 1080p. Closer feels bigger but shows pixels.
  5. Bias light: a dim LED strip behind the screen reduces eye strain in the pitch-dark room without hitting the image.
